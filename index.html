What it touches

Backups reads your projects. It never edits them.

Your originals are only ever read. Everything Backups writes goes to the backup drive you picked, plus one small history file of its own.

Reads

  • The project folders you add in Setup
  • The sample libraries you point it at, to find missing sounds

Writes

  • Dated snapshots on the backup drive or NAS you choose
  • Its own history: %LOCALAPPDATA%\ablebackup on Windows, ~/.ablebackup on Mac and Linux
  • A restored copy, only into a folder you pick

Never

  • Modifies, moves or deletes your original projects or samples
  • Uploads your audio anywhere you didn't set up yourself
  • Sends your project names, file list or music to us

When a sample has moved, Backups relinks it inside the backup copy, matched by file size and not just its name. The project on your machine is left exactly as it was.

Uploader, when it ships, reads the bounce folders you choose and sends audio straight from your computer to your own SoundCloud account. It deletes or edits a SoundCloud track only when you click to do so.

How it runs

Everything happens on your computer.

Local engine
The app window talks to a small engine that runs alongside it on 127.0.0.1. That address is only reachable from your own computer, never from your network or the internet.
Locked to the app
Each time the app starts it makes a fresh secret key, and the engine refuses any request without it. Other programs and web pages can't drive it. The engine shuts down when you close the app.
No account
There is nothing to sign up for. Backups works fully offline.
Updates
When it opens, the app checks the official Releases page for a newer version, and nowhere else. On Windows and Linux it downloads the update in the background and asks before restarting; choose "Later" and it installs next time you quit. On a Mac it shows a button to the download page and you install it yourself.
Uninstalling
On Windows use Settings → Apps → LazyCreatives Backups → Uninstall. On a Mac drag the app from Applications to the Bin. On Linux delete the AppImage file. Your backups on your drive are never removed. To clear the app's history as well, delete the history folder listed above.

Installing

Your computer will warn you. Here's why, and how to check the file.

Code-signing certificates are on the roadmap but the beta isn't signed yet. Windows, macOS and Linux all treat a new unsigned app with caution. The warning means your computer doesn't recognise the publisher yet, not that it found anything wrong.

Always download from GitHub. The buttons above go to github.com/LazyCreatives/lazycreatives-backups/releases. Don't run copies from anywhere else. GitHub lists a SHA-256 fingerprint next to each file on that page, and you can compare it with your download as shown below. If the two values match, the file is exactly what was published.

Windows

  1. Optional: check the file in PowerShell: 
    Get-FileHash "$env:USERPROFILE\Downloads\LazyCreatives-Backups-Windows-Setup.exe"
  2. Run the installer. When the blue "Windows protected your PC" screen appears, click More info, check the app name says LazyCreatives Backups, then click Run anyway.

macOS

  1. Optional: check the file in Terminal: 
    shasum -a 256 ~/Downloads/LazyCreatives-Backups-macOS-arm64.dmg
  2. Install. Open the .dmg and drag LazyCreatives Backups into Applications.
  3. Open it the first time. In Applications, right-click the app and choose Open, then Open again. On macOS 15 or later, if you only see Done or Move to Bin, click Done, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway next to LazyCreatives Backups. After that it opens normally.

Linux

  1. Optional: check the file: 
    sha256sum ~/Downloads/LazyCreatives-Backups-Linux-x86_64.AppImage
  2. Make it runnable and start it: 
    chmod +x ~/Downloads/LazyCreatives-Backups-Linux-x86_64.AppImage
~/Downloads/LazyCreatives-Backups-Linux-x86_64.AppImage
    You can also right-click the file, open Properties → Permissions and tick Allow executing file as program. If it won't start on Ubuntu 22.04 or newer, install FUSE support with sudo apt install libfuse2.

Once it's open, add your project folders and pick a backup drive. Nothing is copied until you press Back up.

If anything looks off, such as a different app name, a file from another site, or a fingerprint that doesn't match, don't run it. Let us know instead.

Privacy

No tracking. Two optional connections, both listed here.

The apps contain no analytics, no crash reporting, no ads and no tracking. The beta needs no licence key and never contacts a payment service. The apps only go online for these reasons, and only if you turn them on:

Cloud mirror (optional)
If you choose to mirror backups to your own Dropbox, Google Drive or OneDrive, files go directly from your computer to your account. We never see them or your login.
SoundCloud (Uploader)
Your audio goes directly to SoundCloud. Signing in passes through a small Lazy Creatives service that holds our SoundCloud app secret. It only swaps the sign-in code for your access tokens and keeps nothing. Tokens are stored on your computer. On Windows they are encrypted to your user account; Mac and Linux secure storage is being added before Uploader ships.

We don't collect your email, your project names, your file list or your music. We have no server that could store them.
